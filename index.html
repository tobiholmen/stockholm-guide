My Stockholm Guide

A personal walking tour through the city I call home

Overview 🏰 Gamla Stan 🏙️ Norrmalm & Vasastan 🌳 Östermalm 🌿 Djurgården 🎨 Södermalm 🌊 Kungsholmen ⛴️ Archipelago

Stockholm isn't a city that overwhelms you with famous landmarks. Instead, it's a city best experienced by wandering through its different neighborhoods, each with its own distinct atmosphere.

The easiest way to understand Stockholm is to think of it as a city built around five main districts, each with its own unique personality. You can easily spend half a day — or even a full day — exploring each of them.

🏛️
Gamla Stan sits right in the middle and is the historic heart of Stockholm.
⬆️
North — Norrmalm and Vasastan: the city center, shopping streets, business district and beautiful residential neighborhoods.
➡️
East — Östermalm, Stockholm's elegant district with grand boulevards, fantastic restaurants and cafés. Continue east and reach Djurgården, the museum island.
⬇️
South — Södermalm, the creative and trendier side of Stockholm, full of independent cafés, viewpoints, vintage stores and nightlife.
⬅️
West — Kungsholmen, a quieter residential island with lovely waterfront promenades and parks, home to Stadshuset (City Hall).

One of the things I love most about Stockholm is that every district has its own identity, yet they're all connected by beautiful bridges, ferries (many of which can be used with a regular SL public transport ticket and make a fantastic, inexpensive sightseeing tour 😄) and excellent public transportation. Walking between neighborhoods is often just as rewarding as the places you're walking to — a big part of Stockholm's charm is everything in between.

Getting Around 🚲

Stockholm has excellent public transportation, and the Tunnelbana (metro) is often called one of the world's most beautiful subway systems thanks to its colorful underground stations. It's a great way to get around the city.

That said, if you're visiting during the warmer months, I'd actually recommend renting a Voi bike or e-bike instead. Stockholm has fantastic bike infrastructure, and cycling between neighborhoods is one of the best ways to experience the city. You'll discover beautiful waterfronts, parks and neighborhoods that you'd completely miss underground. If cycling isn't your thing, Voi's electric scooters are another fun and convenient option for shorter trips.

🏰 Gamla Stan

Stockholm's Old Town is, of course, a must-see and the historic heart of the city.

Take your time wandering through the narrow cobblestone streets, visit Stortorget, and don't miss the Royal Palace. Even if you're not usually interested in royal history, the palace is absolutely worth a visit. Before entering Gamla Stan, it's also worth passing by the Swedish Parliament (Riksdag), one of the country's most important political buildings, beautifully situated between the Old Town and the city center.

The busiest street is Västerlånggatan, which is packed with souvenir shops and tourists. It's fun to walk down once, but don't judge Gamla Stan by this street alone.

For a more local atmosphere, head to Stora Nygatan, where you'll find plenty of cozy pubs and restaurants. This is where many Stockholmers come for a beer or dinner.

Another part that visitors often miss is the eastern side of Gamla Stan around Österlånggatan and Skeppsbron. It's usually much quieter and offers lovely waterfront views. I also recommend stopping by Kornhamnstorg, which has recently been transformed into one of the nicest waterfront spots in Gamla Stan, with outdoor seating and beautiful views across the water to Södermalm. On a sunny day, it's a wonderful place to sit outside with a coffee, beer or ice cream and simply watch the boats go by.

Food Recommendations 🍽️

If you want to try traditional Swedish cuisine, Tradition is one of my favorite hidden gems in the city. It's an excellent introduction to classics like meatballs, herring and other Swedish comfort food.

If you're looking for something a little more playful, Aifur is a Viking-inspired restaurant where the staff stay fully in character. It's definitely touristy, but in the best possible way, and well worth considering if you want a memorable evening.

🏙️ Norrmalm & Vasastan

Norrmalm is Stockholm's city centre and the best place to start exploring the city. Here you'll find the main shopping streets, many of the city's museums, and some of Stockholm's busiest public squares.

Don't miss:

  • Sergels Torg
  • Hötorget
  • Drottninggatan (the main shopping street)
  • Biblioteksgatan (Stockholm's luxury shopping street)
  • NK, Sweden's iconic department store
  • Kungsträdgården & Humlegården, two of the city's most popular parks

If you're looking for shopping, this is where you'll spend most of your time.

A classic Swedish fika ☕

Before continuing your walk, stop by Vetekatten, one of Stockholm's oldest and most beloved cafés. Open since 1928, it's the perfect place to experience a traditional Swedish fika with a coffee and a freshly baked kanelbulle. Despite its history, it's still very affordable and remains a favourite among locals.

Walk towards Vasastan

One of my favourite walks starts at Drottninggatan and continues north until you reach Observatorielunden, a small hill with beautiful views over the rooftops of Stockholm.

From there, continue past Odenplan into Vasastan, one of Stockholm's most charming residential neighbourhoods. Unlike Gamla Stan, you'll hardly find any tourists here — it's where you'll get a glimpse of everyday life in Stockholm. There is also a beautiful park called Vasaparken.

During summer, Rörstrandsgatan becomes one of the city's liveliest streets. The entire street is pedestrianised and lined with cafés, wine bars and restaurants. It's one of my favourite places to spend a warm summer evening.

Restaurants & Bars 🍽️

If you're looking for a classic Swedish dining experience, Tennstopet is one of Stockholm's true institutions. It's traditional without feeling overly formal, serving fantastic Swedish classics in a beautiful historic setting.

Nomad is one of my favourite smaller restaurants. The atmosphere is relaxed, the food is excellent, and during summer they often host concerts in their courtyard. Maybe my favorite meatballs in the city.

For modern Swedish cuisine inspired by northern Sweden, I can highly recommend Knut, which has two locations in the city.

An absolute insider tip is Bar Nordic, which offers food and drinks from northern Sweden. It is actually a vineyard that produces amazing dry wines from berries. The food is great, the staff is super friendly, and it is a wonderful place for a tasting.

For pizza, 800 Grader, Meno Male & Savoy is excellent.

When on Rörstrandsgatan if you're looking for a cosy wine bar, Non Solo Bar is a great choice, while Melkvist Matbar is a great neighbourhood restaurant, or experience amazing fancy kebab at M.O.A.S. (meat on a stick) — actually my favorite on this street.

Need a quick lunch while exploring? K25 is Stockholm's best food hall, offering everything from ramen to tacos, burgers and poke bowls.

For dinner, Asian Post Office serves some of the city's best modern Asian cuisine.

🌳 Östermalm

If Södermalm is Stockholm's creative heart, then Östermalm is its elegant side. Think beautiful architecture, boutique shopping, grand boulevards and some of the city's best restaurants and cafés. It's one of my favourite neighbourhoods simply to wander around without a plan.

A perfect walk through Östermalm

Start at Berzelii Park, a beautiful little park next to the Royal Dramatic Theatre.

Just a few minutes away you'll reach Stureplan, one of Stockholm's best-known squares and the centre of the city's luxury shopping, nightlife and dining scene. The historic Sturegallerian shopping arcade is also located here. At the moment, much of the area is undergoing a major redevelopment, so parts of it may still be hidden behind construction fences depending on when you visit. Even so, it's worth passing through, as this is one of Stockholm's most iconic neighbourhoods.

From there, continue along Grev Turegatan, an elegant street, lined with stylish cafés, restaurants and designer boutiques.

Just around the corner is Östermalms Saluhall, Stockholm's historic food market and one of the city's true culinary landmarks. Even if you're not hungry, it's worth stepping inside just to admire the beautiful building and browse the different food stalls.

From the food hall, continue towards Strandvägen, arguably Stockholm's most beautiful boulevard. Walking along the waterfront towards Djurgårdsbron is one of the most popular walks anywhere in the city.

On the way, stop by Stora Bageriet for excellent coffee and pastries.

Once you reach Djurgårdsbron, you have two options:

  • Turn right and continue into Djurgården, Stockholm's museum island and urban national park.
  • Turn left and walk along Narvavägen towards Karlaplan. From there, continue down the grand boulevard Karlavägen until you reach Humlegården.

Restaurants & Cafés 🍽️

If you're looking for classic Swedish cuisine, Grodan is a true Stockholm institution with a beautiful dining room and fantastic traditional dishes.

Just around the corner, Napolyon Bistro & Bar serves excellent French-inspired cuisine with a relaxed Stockholm atmosphere.

Brasserie Astoria is another Stockholm classic, combining beautiful interiors with outstanding food in a lively setting.

For a more contemporary Swedish dining experience, Restaurang Hantverket is a great one. It has become incredibly popular over the years, so booking well in advance is highly recommended.

If you're in the mood for pizza, Meno Male is my personal favourite in Stockholm.

For a classic Swedish fika, Tössebageriet is another institution that's particularly famous for its semlor during spring.

Craft Beer 🍺

If you're into craft beer, don't miss Omnipollos Flora, located inside Humlegården. Omnipollo is Stockholm's most famous craft brewery, and Flora is their beautiful seasonal beer garden in the middle of the park.

🌿 Djurgården

Djurgården is Stockholm's green heart and probably the city's most unique district. It's hard to believe you're still in the middle of a European capital. The island is home to beautiful parks, waterfront promenades, cafés and many of Stockholm's most famous museums. If you only have time for one museum day, this is where I'd spend it.

Museums

Djurgården has something for almost everyone.

The Vasa Museum is Stockholm's most famous museum and, in my opinion, the one museum you shouldn't skip. It houses the remarkably well-preserved 17th-century warship Vasa, which sank on its maiden voyage and was recovered over 300 years later.

The Nordic Museum is just as impressive from the outside as it is inside. The building itself looks almost like a Harry Potter castle and tells the story of Swedish life and traditions through the centuries.

Other museums worth considering include:

  • ABBA The Museum — surprisingly interactive and a lot of fun, even if you're not a huge ABBA fan.
  • The Viking Museum — a great introduction to Viking history.
  • The Wreck Museum — a smaller museum about shipwrecks in the Baltic Sea.
  • The Spirits Museum — a fun museum exploring Swedish drinking culture, spirits and food.

If you're travelling with children, Skansen is a fantastic combination of open-air museum and zoo, while Gröna Lund is Stockholm's classic amusement park.

The perfect walk

After visiting a museum, simply enjoy the island itself.

One of my favourite routes is to walk through the southern part of Djurgården towards Rosendal.

On the way, it's worth making a small detour to Prins Eugens Waldemarsudde. Even if you don't visit the museum itself, the sculpture garden and waterfront are freely accessible and offer beautiful views over Stockholm.

Another lovely stop is Café Ektorpet, a cosy café tucked away among the trees.

Eventually you'll arrive at Rosendals Trädgård, which, for me, is the real highlight of Djurgården.

Set inside a beautiful garden with greenhouses, it's much more than a café. Grab a coffee and a cinnamon bun, sit beneath the apple trees and simply enjoy the atmosphere. It's one of those places that perfectly captures the slower pace of Swedish life.

From Rosendal, I usually return via Rosendalsvägen and follow the northern waterfront back towards Djurgårdsbron. The walk along the water is beautiful throughout and offers some of my favourite views in the city.

Restaurants & Cafés 🍽️

Near the museums you'll find Blå Porten, a true Stockholm classic serving traditional Swedish food and fika in a beautiful historic setting. It's particularly popular for lunch after visiting the nearby museums.

If you're looking for another coffee stop close to Djurgårdsbron, Stockholm 1897 has quickly become a favourite among locals. I've heard they make some of the best cinnamon buns in Stockholm — but I'll leave that verdict for you to decide. Besides being a bakery and café, it's also a lovely place to sit by the water. Or have a beer at Strandhäxan next to it, it's a little more party vibe, but I really like the location.

🎨 Södermalm

Södermalm is Stockholm's creative heart. It's the city's most vibrant neighbourhood, full of independent cafés, vintage stores, craft beer bars, excellent restaurants and some of Stockholm's best viewpoints. If Östermalm feels elegant, Södermalm feels relaxed and effortlessly cool.

A perfect walk through Södermalm

Start at Slussen, one of Stockholm's biggest redevelopment projects in recent decades. Much of the area has recently been rebuilt into a beautiful new waterfront district with restaurants, terraces and public spaces overlooking the water. It's quickly becoming one of the city's most exciting meeting places.

From Slussen, walk up Götgatan, Södermalm's main shopping street, before turning west towards Hornsgatan.

Hornsgatan has a completely different character. It's known for its independent shops, second-hand boutiques, cafés, bars and nightlife and gives a great impression of Södermalm's more alternative side.

From there, continue onto Bellmansgatan, one of Stockholm's most beautiful residential streets. Fans of Stieg Larsson's Millennium trilogy might recognise it as the neighbourhood where Mikael Blomkvist lives.

At the end of Bellmansgatan, turn left onto Bastugatan, where you'll reach Monteliusvägen, one of Stockholm's most famous viewpoints. The panorama over Riddarfjärden, City Hall and Kungsholmen is spectacular and easily one of the city's best photo spots.

From Monteliusvägen, make your way towards Mariatorget, one of Södermalm's most charming squares.

Continue along Swedenborgsgatan, which during summer becomes completely pedestrianised and transforms into one long outdoor dining street. It's one of my favourite places in Stockholm to spend a warm evening.

If you continue east, you'll eventually reach Medborgarplatsen, Södermalm's main square. The nearby market hall, Söderhallarna, is currently being redeveloped, but the area remains one of Stockholm's busiest neighbourhoods.

From here, head into SoFo ("South of Folkungagatan"), Stockholm's most creative district. Around Nytorget you'll find independent boutiques, design stores, cafés and some of the city's best restaurants. It's the kind of neighbourhood where it's best to simply wander without a destination.

And then you can go down Södermannagatan until you reach Katarina kyrka. Finish your walk at Mosebacke, where Mosebacketerrassen offers one of Stockholm's nicest places to enjoy a drink while watching the sun set over the city.

Restaurants & Cafés 🍽️

Södermalm probably has Stockholm's highest concentration of great cafés and restaurants, so it's almost impossible to go wrong.

Some of my favourites include:

  • Morfar Ginko — excellent pizza and a lively outdoor atmosphere during summer.
  • Bar Agrikultur — one of Stockholm's best contemporary restaurants.
  • Bistro Bananas — great pizza and drinks and good to sit outside on a summer evening.
  • Mormors Dumplings — probably Stockholm's most popular dumpling restaurant.
  • 800 Grader Slice Shop — incredible pizza, arguably better than Bananas, though it's primarily a take-away spot rather than a dine-in restaurant.
  • Woodstockholm — exceptional food and perfect for a longer dinner. The menu is highly creative and always centered around a unique theme.

For coffee and pastries:

  • Lykke Kaffegårdar
  • Pom & Flora
  • Kaferang
  • Omayma
  • Krümel (for outstanding cookies)

Craft Beer & Cocktails 🍺

If you're into cocktails, Stockholms Bränneri is one of the city's absolute highlights. They distil their own gin and have created one of Stockholm's best cocktail bars inside their distillery.

Craft beer lovers are spoiled for choice throughout Södermalm, with countless bars scattered across the neighbourhood, especially Stigbergets Fot, Mikkeller & Akkurat. A great rooftop cocktail bar is Freja, or maybe you like a great gin at Härnö gin bar?

Museums

The standout museum here is Fotografiska, one of the world's best museums dedicated to contemporary photography.

Even if you don't visit the exhibitions, it's worth stopping by the restaurant on the top floor. It offers beautiful waterfront views and serves excellent food, making it a perfect place for lunch or dinner.

My tip

Don't rush Södermalm. Unlike Gamla Stan or Djurgården, there isn't one single attraction that defines the district. The charm lies in wandering through its streets, discovering small cafés, sitting outside with a coffee or beer and simply soaking up the atmosphere. It's the neighbourhood where Stockholm feels most alive.

🌊 Kungsholmen

Kungsholmen is often overlooked by visitors, but it's one of the best places to experience everyday life in Stockholm. The island is quieter than Södermalm or Gamla Stan, yet offers beautiful waterfront promenades, parks and cafés. It's the kind of place where Stockholmers come for an evening walk, a swim or dinner by the water.

The Waterfront Walk

Start at Stockholm City Hall (Stadshuset), arguably Stockholm's most iconic landmark.

Even if you've seen it from the outside, I highly recommend joining one of the guided tours. They don't take very long and allow you to visit the magnificent Blue Hall, where the Nobel Prize banquet is held each year, and the spectacular Golden Hall, covered in millions of golden mosaic tiles. The tour also offers a fascinating insight into Stockholm's political history and one of the city's most remarkable buildings.

Before leaving, spend a few minutes in the gardens behind City Hall. The waterfront views over Riddarfjärden and Södermalm are among the most beautiful in Stockholm. Right next door, Café Eldkvarn is a perfect place for a coffee — or even a beer — before continuing your walk.

From City Hall, simply follow Norr Mälarstrand, one of Stockholm's most beloved waterfront promenades. On a sunny day you'll find runners, cyclists, families, people swimming and countless Stockholmers simply enjoying the water.

As you continue west, you'll eventually reach Mälarpaviljongen, a floating restaurant and café built directly on the water. It's one of Stockholm's nicest spots for lunch or an afternoon drink and serves excellent Swedish classics, including great meatballs.

Continue towards Rålambshovsparken, one of Stockholm's largest parks. During summer it's full of picnics, outdoor sports and people relaxing by the water.

If you're in the mood for a drink, make a short detour to Eden, a stylish restaurant and bar overlooking the bay. During the day it's a relaxed place for lunch or cocktails, while in the evening it transforms into one of Stockholm's livelier summer hangouts with music and a great atmosphere.

If you have some extra time, stop at Boulebar Rålambshov, where you can play a game of pétanque while enjoying a beer or dinner.

To return, either walk back along the waterfront or cross the island through Kronobergsparken and the quieter residential streets around Hantverkargatan. It gives you a nice impression of what everyday life on Kungsholmen feels like.

Restaurants 🍽️

Kungsholmen is more residential than the other districts, but there are still some outstanding restaurants.

AG is widely regarded as one of the best steakhouses in Sweden — and arguably one of the best meat restaurants in Europe. If you're a steak lover, it's absolutely worth making a reservation.

For pizza, Deglabbet is another excellent option.

And of course, Mälarpaviljongen deserves another mention — not just for the food, but for its unique location floating on the water.

My tip

Kungsholmen isn't about ticking off famous attractions. It's about slowing down. Grab a coffee at City Hall, walk along the waterfront, have lunch overlooking the lake, enjoy a drink at Eden as the sun starts to set, and simply take in the atmosphere. If you want to understand why Stockholm consistently ranks among the world's most livable cities, Kungsholmen is probably the district that shows it best.

⛴️ Stockholm Archipelago

If you only do one day trip from Stockholm, make it the archipelago.

What makes Stockholm so unique isn't just the city itself, but that it's surrounded by around 30,000 islands, skerries and rocky outcrops stretching all the way into the Baltic Sea. Even better, getting there is incredibly easy. Ferries leave from central Stockholm throughout the day, and many destinations can be reached in under an hour.

For me, the archipelago is where Stockholm really comes alive during summer. People spend weekends in little red cottages, swim from sun-warmed rocks, arrive by sailboat, kayak between islands, or simply enjoy a long lunch overlooking the water. It feels peaceful, relaxed and unmistakably Swedish.

Which island should you visit?

If you only have one day, here are my favourites:

Vaxholm is the classic first-time destination. It's often called the capital of the archipelago, with colourful wooden houses, cafés, restaurants and a lovely harbour. It's lively without feeling crowded and very easy to reach.

Grinda is perfect if you mainly want nature. There are beautiful walking trails, swimming spots, forests and rocky coastlines, making it ideal for a relaxing day outdoors.

Sandhamn is one of the most famous islands and feels almost like a small seaside resort. It's a longer journey but rewards you with sandy beaches, charming wooden houses, sailing culture and some of the most beautiful scenery in the archipelago.

If you're short on time, I'd recommend Fjäderholmarna. It's the closest island in the archipelago to central Stockholm, with regular boats taking only around 30 minutes. Think of it as a miniature version of the archipelago: it's small enough to explore in just a few hours, yet still offers everything that makes the islands so special. You'll find craft workshops, a small brewery, cafés, restaurants and plenty of rocky waterfront where you can simply sit and enjoy the sea. If you're visiting Stockholm for a long weekend or only have half a day to spare, this is probably the easiest way to experience the archipelago without committing to a full-day trip.

If you're there in the evening, walk down to Röda Villan, the beautiful villa restaurant at the southern end of the island. It has a wonderful terrace overlooking the water and is one of my favourite places to watch the evening sun with dinner or a drink before taking the ferry back to the city. It makes for a perfect, relaxed end to the day.

If you have more time, simply island-hop. That's one of the best parts of visiting the archipelago.

Getting there ⛴️

The journey is part of the experience.

Several ferry companies operate routes from central Stockholm, but I usually recommend Waxholmsbolaget. Their ferries are part of Stockholm's public transport network and offer a much more authentic experience than the dedicated sightseeing boats.

Try to sit outside if the weather is nice. Cruising past tiny islands, summer houses, forests and sailboats is every bit as memorable as arriving at your destination.

My tip

Don't overplan. The best days in the archipelago are often the simplest ones: take the ferry, wander around without a schedule, go for a swim if the weather allows, have lunch overlooking the water, buy an ice cream, and enjoy the slow pace of island life.

It's the perfect reminder that one of Stockholm's greatest attractions isn't a building or a museum — it's the incredible nature that's only a ferry ride away.
